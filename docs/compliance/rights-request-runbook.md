# Individual rights request runbook

Status: operational draft for launch preparation. Obtain legal review before
using this as the business's final procedure.

## Intake

Customers, gift recipients and visitors can submit the public privacy request
form. Each accepted request receives a reference and a due date one calendar
month after receipt. Account holders may also download account data or request
account closure from account settings.

The Admin **Privacy requests** queue is the system of record for:

- the request type and requester relationship;
- contact and order-reference information supplied by the requester;
- identity-verification state;
- case status, internal notes, receipt date and response deadline.

Do not copy card details, passwords, gift messages or unnecessary sensitive
information into internal notes.

## Triage and identity verification

1. Review new cases each working day and move them to **Identity check**.
2. Confirm that the requested right and records are sufficiently clear.
3. Use proportionate verification. Prefer information already known to the
   requester, such as control of the account email or limited order details.
   Do not request photo identification by default.
4. For a gift recipient, verify only the minimum delivery or contact details
   needed to locate their data. Do not disclose purchaser identity, gift
   messages or other third-party information without a lawful basis.
5. Record the verification result, not copies of identity evidence. Mark the
   identity as **Verified** or **Failed** and explain the decision in notes.
6. The response clock can be paused only where the law permits while genuinely
   necessary identity information is outstanding. Seek advice before changing
   the displayed deadline.

## Handling by right

### Access

Search the customer profile, addresses, orders, gift-order details, delivery
reservations, wishlist, occasions, preferences, reviews and relevant processor
records. Remove third-party information and privileged/confidential material
before disclosure. Account export JSON is a starting point, not an automatic
final response.

### Correction

Correct inaccurate live data through the normal Admin or account workflow.
Where a legally retained order record must not be rewritten, attach a clear
correction note or linked record. Notify processors where necessary.

### Deletion

Account closure removes the active profile, saved addresses, preferences,
wishlist and occasions, and anonymises review attribution. Order, payment,
refund, tax, delivery and gift-recipient evidence may be retained only where a
legal obligation, legal claim or other documented ground applies. Record each
retained category and its reason in the response. Apply approved processor and
backup procedures.

### Restriction

Stop non-essential use while the request is assessed. Record which processing
is restricted, notify relevant staff/processors and prevent marketing or
personalisation use. Preserve data needed to establish or defend legal claims.

### Objection

Identify the processing and lawful basis. Stop direct marketing immediately.
For legitimate-interest processing, stop unless documented compelling grounds
or legal claims apply. Consent withdrawal must also update cookie or marketing
preferences.

### Portability

Provide customer-supplied and observed account/order data processed by consent
or contract in a structured, commonly used format. The account JSON export is
the default starting format. Exclude inferred data and third-party information.

## Completion

1. Have a second authorised person review disclosures, refusals and retained
   data where possible.
2. Send the response through the verified contact channel.
3. Record the response date and a concise outcome; do not paste the full
   disclosure into notes.
4. Mark the case **Completed**, or **Rejected** only with a documented reason
   and complaint route.
5. Explain the right to complain to the Information Commissioner's Office.
6. Retain the case log according to `RIGHTS-01` in the retention schedule.

## Deadline and escalation

The normal deadline is one calendar month from receipt. Escalate a case
immediately if it is close to the deadline, involves multiple identities,
requires processor searches, appears manifestly unfounded/excessive, or may
need an extension. Any extension or refusal needs a documented legal basis and
timely notice to the requester.
